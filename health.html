1. Your listings (Google, Bing, Apple and the rest)

When someone searches "pool builder near me", these matter even more than your website.

3. What Google needs from your website and isn't getting

  • Google can't read your business details. Websites can include hidden information that tells Google "this is a pool builder at this address, with these hours and this phone number". Yours doesn't have it. Your SEO plugin (Yoast) is installed but not set up for this. It's a one-time fix.
  • 57 of your 82 pages have no description. That's the short text under your link in Google results, so right now Google makes one up. Every blog post is missing one.
  • 44 pages have no main heading, including every blog post, so Google has to guess what each post is about.
  • 520 of 605 images (86%) have no alt text. That's the description that helps Google Images and people using screen readers. None of the 7 images on your home page have it.
  • Thin, repeat pages show up in Google: 8 tag pages, an "Uncategorized" category and author pages are mostly the same list of posts. Yoast can hide them.
  • Repeated page titles: your home page shares a title with another page, and two Webster pages share one.

What's working: secure HTTPS, with the www and .net addresses forwarding properly. Your sitemap and robots file are fine, missing pages show a proper "not found" page, and Google Analytics is installed.

  • It takes 14 seconds for the big photo at the top of your home page to show up on a phone. Google wants it under 2.5 seconds, and most people leave long before 14.

  • The fix: shrink and compress the top photo, switch to a lighter image format (WebP), add a caching plugin, and cut back the page-builder add-ons.

5. Email

  • Your email runs on southerncharmpools.net through Microsoft 365. Your website is on .com, which uses a different mail service.
  • Neither domain has a DMARC record. Without it, anyone can send an email that looks like it's from you, and Gmail and Yahoo trust your mail less. It's a 10-minute fix in your domain settings.
  • Worth a quick test: send yourself a message through your website's contact form at a Gmail address and make sure it doesn't land in spam.

6. Security (a light look from the outside)

  • You have a Sucuri firewall, and it blocks the usual WordPress snooping. That's good.
  • Your admin username is public. The blog author pages are at /author/megasiteadmin/. Set a display name and turn off author pages.
  • A file called /readme.html shows which WordPress version you run. Delete it.
  • The browser security settings (HSTS and similar) are off. Your host or Sucuri can turn them on with one setting.
  • Your security certificate is good until February 2027.
