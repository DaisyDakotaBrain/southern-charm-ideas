1. Listings (Google, Bing, Apple and the rest)

These matter more than the website for "pool builder near me" searches.

Google Business Profile

  • 4.9 stars from 114 reviews. Category "Swimming pool contractor", address and phone are correct.
  • Hours don't match. Google says Mon to Fri, 8:30 AM to 5:30 PM. The website header says 9 AM to 5 PM. Pick one and make them match everywhere.
  • The site and the redesign say "105 reviews". That's now out of date; it should be 114.
  • The profile shows an "Own this business?" link to the public. They post updates, so it's probably verified, but confirm someone in the family has owner access, not only the web vendor.
  • Profile links point to X (Twitter), Instagram and YouTube. The website's own links to Twitter don't work (see section 2).

Apple Maps (iPhone users, Siri, CarPlay)

  • Unclaimed. The listing shows "Claim This Place". Apple fills it in from Yelp.
  • City shows as Kemah instead of Clear Lake Shores.
  • No photos, no hours, no description of their own.
  • Fix: claim it free at Apple Business Connect (businessconnect.apple.com), correct the city, and add hours, logo, photos and a description.

Bing Places (Bing, Windows search, ChatGPT and Copilot answers)

  • Claimed and verified. Address and phone are correct.
  • Wrong services listed: Chimney Construction, Carpet Repair, Water Heaters. Remove them; add pool construction, remodels, outdoor kitchens and equipment repair.
  • Hours open 8:30 AM: matches Google, not the website.
  • Fix: log in at bingplaces.com (it can import straight from Google Business Profile).

BBB

  • A+ rating, correct address and phone.
  • The listing doesn't show BBB accreditation. Saying "BBB A+" is fine; don't call it "accredited" unless they've paid for accreditation. (The redesign says "accredited rating" and needs that word changed.)

Directory cleanup

  • Wheree.com lists three wrong addresses (5201 McCart Ave; 1010 Marina Bay Dr; 1002 Aspen Rd). Old addresses confuse Google about where the business is.
  • Facebook: search shows a second "Southern Charm Pools" page that talks about "pilas" and serving "all of South Texas" with "over 20 years" in the business. It may be another company with the same name. Confirm which page is theirs, and report it to Facebook if it's pretending to be them.
  • Houzz: 4.8 from 6 reviews. Fine, but worth asking a few happy customers to post there too.
  • Yelp blocked our check. Look at it by hand, since Apple Maps copies whatever Yelp says.

3. Website: what Google needs and isn't getting

  • No LocalBusiness schema anywhere. This is the behind-the-scenes info that tells Google "this is a pool builder at this address, with these hours and this phone number". Yoast (their SEO plugin) is installed but only marks pages as "web page". A one-time setup.
  • 57 of 82 pages have no meta description, which is the text that shows under the link in Google results. That includes every blog post.
  • 44 pages have no H1 heading. Every blog post is missing its main heading, so Google has to guess what each post is about.
  • 520 of 605 images (86%) have no alt text. That hurts Google Images and screen-reader users. The home page has 7 images and none have alt text.
  • Thin and duplicate pages are indexable: 8 tag pages, an "Uncategorized" category and author archives are mostly the same list of posts. Set them to noindex in Yoast.
  • Duplicate page titles: the home page and another page share a title, and two Webster pages share one.

What's working: HTTPS and the www and .net redirects are all set up correctly, there's a sitemap, robots.txt is fine, missing pages return a proper 404, and Google Analytics 4 is installed.

4. Website speed (Google PageSpeed, mobile)

  • Largest Contentful Paint: 14.0 seconds on a phone. That's how long until the big hero image shows. Google wants under 2.5 seconds, and most visitors leave long before 14.
  • The home page is 4 MB. Render-blocking files cost about 2 seconds, and missing cache settings waste 1.5 MB on repeat visits.
  • The SEO score of 100 only covers basic checks. It doesn't catch the missing schema, descriptions and headings above.
  • Fix: compress and resize the hero image, use WebP, add a caching plugin, and trim the Elementor add-ons.

5. Email

  • Email runs on southerncharmpools.net through Microsoft 365. The website is on .com, which uses a different mail host.
  • Neither domain has a DMARC record. Without it, anyone can send email that looks like it's from them, and Gmail and Yahoo are stricter with domains that don't have one. A 10-minute DNS fix (start with p=none reporting).
  • Worth a test: send the website contact form to Gmail and make sure it doesn't land in spam.

6. Security (light, public-only look)

  • A Sucuri firewall is in place and blocks the usual WordPress snooping. Good.
  • The admin username is public (the author pages are at /author/megasiteadmin/). Set an author display name and remove author archives.
  • /readme.html shows the WordPress version. Delete it.
  • No browser security headers (HSTS, X-Frame-Options and so on). The host or Sucuri can add these in one setting.
  • The SSL certificate is valid until February 2027.
