Starting Southern Charm Pool Care: my plan for you

Here's how I'd add a weekly pool cleaning service to Southern Charm. It would run under your name (4.9 stars, building pools in Clear Lake Shores and League City since 2016), with Kody leading it day to day. Anything marked (est.) is my best guess, so please check those numbers before you count on them.

1. Why I think this works for you

  • You already have the customers. Every pool you've built is a warm lead. Those owners already trust you, and your crew installed their equipment.
  • It feeds work in both directions. Weekly visits catch failing pumps, heaters and leaks early, and that turns into repair and remodel jobs for the build side.
  • It pays every month. Construction comes in waves. A service route pays every month, all year, since Gulf Coast pools run 12 months.
  • Your site doesn't offer it yet. southerncharmpools.com never mentions weekly maintenance, so this fills a gap instead of competing with what you already do.

3. Legal, licensing and insurance (please verify each one)

  • [ ] Register the business: an assumed name certificate (DBA) or a new LLC with the Texas Secretary of State.
  • [ ] Sales tax: ask your CPA or the Texas Comptroller how pool service and chemicals sold are taxed, and whether you need a sales tax permit. I wouldn't guess on this one.
  • [ ] Algaecide rules: check with the Texas Department of Agriculture whether applying algaecide as a paid service needs a pesticide applicator license.
  • [ ] Certification: a CPO (Certified Pool Operator) course for Kody. It isn't required for homes, but it's two days, builds trust, and you'd need it to ever service HOA or community pools.
  • [ ] Insurance: general liability, commercial auto on the service truck, and ask your agent about chemical and pollution coverage. Workers' comp once you hire.
  • [ ] Hauling chemicals: liquid chlorine and muriatic acid ride in the truck. Ask about small-quantity hazmat rules and set up locked, separate storage.
  • [ ] Service agreement: a one-page month-to-month agreement covering gate access, pets, what's included, what costs extra, payment and canceling.

4. Equipment and tools (est. $4k to $8k to start, not counting the truck)

  • Truck or trailer setup with locked chemical storage
  • A professional drop test kit, plus a digital reader for the photo reports
  • Poles, brushes, leaf rakes, vacuum heads and hoses, a leaf canister
  • Filter-cleaning gear and a salt cell cleaning setup
  • Starting chemicals: bulk liquid chlorine, acid, tabs, stabilizer, salt
  • Uniform shirts and a truck decal (cheap, and it advertises on every street)

Rough route math (all guesses): - One tech can do about 12 to 18 full-service stops a day, so a full week is about 60 to 80 pools. - 60 pools × $215 = about $12,900 a month. - Chemicals: about $25 to $40 per pool a month, so $1,500 to $2,400. - Fuel, truck, insurance, supplies: maybe $1,500 to $2,500 a month. - That leaves roughly $8,000 to $9,900 a month at 60 pools, before Kody's pay and taxes. Recheck once real chemical and fuel costs come in.

Extras that pay well: green-to-clean recoveries, filter cleans, storm cleanups, startups on your new builds, and repair jobs sent back to the build crew.

A shortcut to grow: established pool routes get bought and sold, usually for a multiple of their monthly revenue. Good to know about once you're up and running.

6. The app (Charm Route)

I built a working demo of an app for running the route, so you wouldn't have to pay for Skimmer or Pool Brain. It does what they do (routes, chemical logs, photo reports to customers) plus things they can't: - It pulls each pool's equipment and warranties from your build records. - It sends problems straight to the build crew, or turns them into remodel leads. - It coaches the tech on dosing and spotting problems, so even someone new to pool care can run a route safely from week one.

It runs on any phone as a web app, with no App Store needed. Before you'd use it for real, it still needs logins, a database, saving visits when there's no signal, billing, and someone to keep it running. I'd try it on 5 pools for 2 weeks before moving the whole route onto it.

7. Getting your first customers (weeks 4 to 10)

  1. Your past build customers. Text or email every one: "Your first month of weekly service is on us." 15 to 25 signups from this list alone is a realistic goal.
  2. Warranty and repair calls. End every service call with a pool care offer.
  3. Neighbors of your builds. Door hangers on streets where you've built. One stop on a street makes the next one there almost free.
  4. Google Business Profile. Add the pool care service and ask every early customer for a review. This is the biggest source of local leads for home services.
  5. Neighborhood Facebook groups and Nextdoor. League City and Clear Lake groups are active, and "who does your pool?" posts come up all the time.
  6. Real estate agents. People buying homes with pools need service right away.

8. Website

  • What I made: a Pool Care page as part of the website redesign, using your own photos and logo.
  • For launch: either (a) a Pool Care section on southerncharmpools.com, which keeps your Google ranking and reviews in one place, or (b) its own domain that links back to the main site. I'd go with (a) unless you set up a separate LLC.
  • Must haves: a quote form that actually reaches the office, tap-to-call, service area pages, the behind-the-scenes business info Google reads, and your Google reviews on the page.
