Southern Charm Pool Care: Launch Outline

Working plan for spinning a weekly pool cleaning service out of Southern Charm Pools (custom pool builder, Clear Lake Shores / League City TX, est. 2016, 4.9 stars across ~105 reviews). Kody would run it. Numbers marked (est.) are planning assumptions to verify, not quotes.

1. Why this works for them

  • Built-in customer list. Every pool they've built is a warm lead. Those owners already trust the brand and already own equipment the crew installed.
  • Cross-sell both directions. Service visits catch failing pumps, heaters and leaks early, and that becomes repair and remodel work for the parent company.
  • Recurring revenue. Construction is lumpy. A service route pays every month, all year (Gulf Coast pools run 12 months).
  • The parent site doesn't offer it. southerncharmpools.com never mentions weekly maintenance, so this fills a real gap rather than competing with itself.

3. Legal, licensing, insurance (verify each)

  • [ ] Business registration: DBA (assumed name certificate) or new LLC with the Texas SOS.
  • [ ] Sales tax: ask the CPA / Texas Comptroller how residential pool service vs. chemicals sold are taxed in Texas, and whether a sales tax permit is needed. Don't guess here.
  • [ ] Algaecide / chemical rules: confirm with the Texas Dept. of Agriculture whether applying algaecides as a paid service needs a pesticide applicator license.
  • [ ] Certification: CPO (Certified Pool Operator) for Kody. Not required for homes, but it's a 2-day course, builds credibility, and is required if they ever service HOA/community pools.
  • [ ] Insurance: general liability, commercial auto on the service truck, and ask the agent about chemical/pollution coverage. Workers' comp once they hire.
  • [ ] Chemical transport and storage: liquid chlorine and muriatic acid in the truck. Ask about small-quantity hazmat rules and get proper secured storage.
  • [ ] Service agreement: one-page month-to-month terms (access, pets, what's included, what's extra, payment, cancellation).

4. Equipment and tools (est. $4k-8k to start, excluding the truck)

  • Truck or trailer setup with locked chemical storage
  • Test kit (a professional drop-test kit, plus a digital reader for the photo reports)
  • Poles, brushes, leaf rakes, vacuum heads and hoses, a leaf canister
  • Filter-cleaning gear (cartridge wand / DE supplies), salt cell cleaning setup
  • Starting chemical inventory (bulk liquid chlorine, acid, tabs, stabilizer, salt)
  • Uniform shirts and a truck decal (cheap, and it advertises on every street)

Proposed pricing (shown on the Pool Care page):

Plan Price Includes Position
Chemical check $139/mo Weekly test and balance, chemicals, baskets Just under Blue Science / True and Blue
Full service $215/mo + brush, vacuum, skim, equipment check, photo report Matches Admiral, under True and Blue and Pool Boys

Base prices cover pools up to ~20,000 gal. Price larger pools, spas and heavy tree cover at the quote (+$20-40/mo is typical). Consider a one-time startup fee (~$100-150) for non-Southern Charm pools, and waive it for past build customers.

Example route math (all assumptions): - A solo tech can do roughly 12-18 full-service stops a day, so a full week is ~60-80 pools. - 60 pools × $215 = ~$12,900/mo revenue. - Chemicals ~$25-40 per pool/mo → ~$1,500-2,400. - Fuel, truck, insurance, software, supplies → maybe $1,500-2,500/mo. - That leaves roughly $8k-9.9k/mo before Kody's pay and taxes at 60 pools. Recheck once real chemical and fuel costs come in.

Extras that pay well: green-to-clean recoveries, filter cleans, storm cleanups, startups for new builds, and repair referrals back to the parent company.

Shortcut to grow: established pool routes are bought and sold, usually priced as a multiple of monthly revenue. Worth knowing as an option once the operation is running.

6. Software

Charm Route (our own app, prototype in app/) instead of Skimmer or Pool Brain. It does what they do (routing, chemical logs, photo reports to customers) plus what they can't: pulls equipment and warranty from Southern Charm's build records, sends problems back to the build crew or turns them into remodel leads, and coaches the tech on dosing and diagnosis, so someone new to pool care can run a route safely from week one. Before launch it still needs: real logins, a database, offline saving for back yards with no signal, billing (Stripe), and someone who owns keeping it running. Run it on 5 pools for 2 weeks before the full route moves onto it.

7. Getting the first customers (weeks 4-10)

  1. Past build customers. Email or text every Southern Charm customer: "Your first month of weekly service is on us." Target: 15-25 signups from this list alone.
  2. Warranty and repair calls. Every service call ends with a pool care offer.
  3. Neighbors of current builds. Door hangers on the streets where they've built (one route stop makes the next one on the same street almost free).
  4. Google Business Profile. Set up a profile for the pool care service and ask every early customer for a review. This is the #1 local lead source for home services.
  5. Neighborhood Facebook groups / Nextdoor. League City and Clear Lake groups are active, and "who does your pool?" posts come up all the time.
  6. Real estate agents and new homeowners. Buyers of homes with pools need service immediately.

8. Website

  • Concept: the Pool Care page in the website redesign (uses your own photos and logo).
  • For launch: either (a) a /pool-care section on southerncharmpools.com (keeps the SEO and reviews in one place), or (b) its own domain like southerncharmpoolcare.com (check availability) that links back. Lean (a) unless they choose a separate LLC.
  • Must have: quote form that actually reaches the office, click-to-call, service area pages, LocalBusiness schema, and Google reviews shown on the page.

10. Open questions for Kody and the owner

  • Separate LLC or a division? Does Kody get equity or a revenue share?
  • How many pools has Southern Charm built since 2016? (Sizes the warm-lead list.)
  • Is there a spare truck, or does this need a vehicle purchase?
  • Can repair work found on routes be handled by the parent crew, and how is it split?
  • Do they want to service pools they didn't build from day one? (Recommended: yes.)
